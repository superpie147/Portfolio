My own problems, defined and built by myself

Life keeps producing moments of “this would be so much easier if a tool for it existed.” Luckily, today’s AI can reproduce what I have in mind at a very low cost in time — so defining what I need and actually building it has become genuinely possible.

Personal project · 2026.09 · PWA (installable on your phone)

What’s for dinner? — fridge-to-recipe suggestions

Cooking is a hobby of mine, but I often open the fridge with no idea what to make. Tick the ingredients you have at home, and the app ranks recipes by two scores — ingredients on hand and seasonings on hand — across Japanese, Taiwanese and simple Western dishes, named in Chinese and Japanese. Ingredients, seasonings and recipes are all editable in a small admin screen, and it installs on a phone’s home screen. The look is a “hand-drawn cooking notebook”: grid paper, handwriting fonts, washi tape and sticky notes. The data layer is isolated so cloud sync can be swapped in later.

Personal project · 2026.07 · Web app × cloud database

100 Small Goals

Every year I write down 100 small goals and work through them over the months. I wanted more than a checklist: a way to keep the photo and the feeling of the moment each one was done, so that by December it reads like a memoir of the year, and can be shared by link. Google sign-in and a Supabase cloud database keep it safe across devices. The rules are “edit freely as a draft, then once published the structure locks while progress stays editable,” with access control enforced in the database layer and a full design → independent verification → fix → re-verify security pass.

Personal project · 2026.07 · Web app (interactive map)

Japan’s 100 Famous Castles — a visit log

Japan’s 100 Famous Castles were selected in 2007 by the Japan Castle Foundation to mark its 40th anniversary: castles of historical value, chosen jointly by historians and architectural scholars. I like visiting these old castles with their sense of history, and I wanted a record of them — not a list, but a map I can open and see where I’ve been. All 100 castles sit on a hand-drawn-style map of Japan; visited ones get a vermilion seal with the date and a note. The data stays in your own browser, can be exported as a backup, and turns into a read-only share link for friends.

If you feel like visiting them yourself, each castle has an official 100 Castles stamp you can collect on site. They’re adorable.

Personal project · 2026 · Automation

Job-hunt automation with Notion × Make

I keep a job-hunt tracker in Notion for every application I send out: the role, its status, the date I applied, and so on. But these days many companies simply never write back, however long you wait (even though we all know that usually means no). So I wired the tracker to the automation tool Make: any application with no movement for 30 days gets its status updated automatically, and I can keep my attention on the ones still in play.

Personal project · 2026.05 · Web app

JLPT N1 study game

Learning through play works better for me than drilling, so I built this game to help myself — and the Japanese learners around me — pick up new material. It’s set in a sushi shop: every correct answer earns a piece of sushi, words you get wrong stay in a “weak words” list for review, and every question shows how all players have scored on it. It has kept evolving since launch based on how it’s actually used. My first attempt at turning an idea into something real with AI.
